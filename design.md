# Website Redesign Notebook: `design.md`

This document tracks our collaborative design decisions, section by section, starting from mood and art direction down to typography, layout, and component craft.

---

## 1. Honest Assessment of the Current Mood

### What the site currently feels like:
- **An Identity Crisis**: A jarring hybrid between a dark SaaS developer tool, a vintage Polaroid scrapbook, and a playful cartoon zoo widget.
- **Surface Clash**: Placing stark ivory Polaroid frames on top of an obsidian grid dark mode (`#141517`) feels like photos floating artificially on a tech terminal, rather than resting in a cohesive physical or editorial space.
- **Typographic Noise**: 4 competing font families on one screen (Fraunces serif, Caveat cursive, JetBrains Mono, Plus Jakarta Sans) creates visual exhaustion instead of clear hierarchy.
- **Overcrowded Hero**: Crammed with status pills, dispatch tags, metric boxes, and controls before the viewer even has a chance to connect with the wildlife.
- **Emotional Resonance**: Currently **cold, disjointed, and synthetic**. It lacks a single, confident emotional point of view.

---

## 2. Setting the Mood: Candidate Directions

Before writing code or styling components, we must lock in the emotional atmosphere. Here are 4 distinct, cohesive directions:

### Direction A: The Sun-Drenched Safari Journal
- **Mood**: Warm, tactile, nostalgic, organic, grounded in the earth.
- **Atmosphere**: The heat of the midday savanna, canvas field tents, unbleached heavy paper, worn leather bindings, dust and golden light.
- **Materials & Palette**: Raw linen/canvas ground, sun-faded parchment (`#F5F2EB`), acacia soot (`#1E1A17`), warm savanna amber, terracotta stamp seals.
- **Why it fits Polaroids**: The Polaroid frames feel entirely authentic here—like physical photos tucked into a traveler’s field notebook with handwritten observations.

### Direction B: The Minimalist Art Monograph (Leica / Fine-Art Gallery)
- **Mood**: Quiet, breathtaking, confident, spacious, museum-grade reverence.
- **Atmosphere**: An exclusive photographic exhibition. Photography is the hero; everything else recedes into whisper-quiet, disciplined elegance.
- **Materials & Palette**: Pure architectural whitespace or deep graphite gallery, refined hairline rules, generous breathing room, zero clutter, zero gimmicks.
- **Why it works**: Eliminates all visual slop and lets the beauty of the wildlife speak for itself with utmost dignity.

### Direction C: 1970s National Geographic Field Expedition
- **Mood**: Analog, authentic, documentary journalism, vintage Kodachrome.
- **Atmosphere**: Heavy brass cameras, yellow borders, typewriter notes, field map coordinates, archival specimen tags.
- **Materials & Palette**: Field olive/khaki, iconic border yellow, warm tobacco brown, weathered ivory.
- **Why it works**: Bold, unmistakable retro character with deep storytelling flavor.

### Direction D: Midnight Savanna & Night Patrol
- **Mood**: Atmospheric, mysterious, nocturnal, solitary.
- **Atmosphere**: The savanna under moonlight and starlight. Silent predators, night camera traps, deep low-light reverence without looking like a generic SaaS dark mode.
- **Materials & Palette**: Deep twilight obsidian, moonlight silver, phosphor amber edge accents.

## 3. Step-by-Step Redesign Roadmap

1. **Step 1: Mood & Art Direction Alignment** (Current step)
   - Choose the emotional core, primary palette, and material world.
2. **Step 2: Typography & Lettering System**
   - Pare down to 1 or 2 deliberate, expressive typefaces that carry the agreed mood.
3. **Step 3: The Hero Experience**
   - Strip the clutter; design a bold, singular opening moment that sets the tone immediately.
4. **Step 4: Sighting Presentation & Photo Framing**
   - Design how the photos and wildlife records live on the page in harmony with the mood.
5. **Step 5: Browsing & Filter Experience**
   - Clean, effortless exploration (search, biome/species filtering) that doesn't feel like a noisy dashboard.
6. **Step 6: Polish, Micro-interactions & Hand-off**
   - Refine spacing, transitions, mobile responsiveness, and tactile details.
